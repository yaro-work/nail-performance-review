# Editable career expectations

These are starting points for a conversation with your manager. They are not a universal ladder, a checklist for promotion, or verified company requirements.

Use your employer's ladder first. Keep the expectations that fit your role, rewrite unclear ones, and remove irrelevant examples.

## Engineering examples

| Illustrative level | Focus to discuss | Evidence you might collect |
|---|---|---|
| Associate | Build confidence through small, reviewed changes. | A finished task and feedback you applied. |
| Mid-level | Take a manageable feature through delivery. | A trade-off you explained and a delivery risk you addressed. |
| Senior | Turn a loosely specified team problem into a dependable result. | Decisions, operational results, and colleagues you helped. |
| Staff | Make complex work easier for a group to deliver. | Stakeholder agreement, reduced recurring friction, and reusable learning. |
| Principal | Guide technical choices across a broader business area. | Cross-team decisions and progress toward longer-term needs. |

These generic prompts were independently rewritten on November 8, 2026, using the public frameworks linked below and in the source list. They are not a reproduction of either company's ladder, and the level names are not an equivalence between companies.

## Company examples differ

Monzo's February 2025 framework emphasizes the scope and impact of work. It describes multiple ways to contribute at senior levels, including domain expertise and project leadership.

GitLab's staff development expectations describe a technical leader for a team's domain. They include enabling others, sound trade-offs, and business understanding.

These examples illustrate why titles cannot be mapped directly between companies. Agree on the expected scope with your manager before using a generic example.

## Examples for other roles

Choose dimensions that match your work. A designer, analyst, program manager, or operations specialist can use the same evidence workflow.

| Dimension | An editable expectation | Useful evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Delivery | Complete agreed work and make risks visible early. | A shipped result, decision record, or stakeholder feedback. |
| Judgment | Explain why a decision supports a real user or business need. | Alternatives considered and the resulting outcome. |
| Collaboration | Help partners make progress and give clear updates. | Shared deliverables and specific feedback. |
| Leadership | Help others act with clearer priorities or better practices. | Adoption, reduced confusion, or teammate growth. |
| Impact | Check whether the work produced its intended result. | Before-and-after measures or a documented qualitative result. |

## Use promotion goals carefully

Ask whether the person wants to discuss progression. Staying effective in their current role is a valid goal.

If they want a promotion, separate current-role expectations from target-role expectations. Ask their manager which evidence and opportunities matter in that company.

Record gaps as opportunities to discuss. Do not turn missing evidence into a rating of the person.
